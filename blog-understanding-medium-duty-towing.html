Understanding Medium Duty Towing Service: What You Need to Know

A practical overview of medium-duty towing, the vehicles it is designed for, and why the right equipment matters.

What is medium-duty towing?

Medium-duty towing is intended for vehicles that are larger or heavier than typical passenger cars and pickups but do not require full heavy-duty recovery equipment. Common examples include box trucks, delivery vans, larger work vehicles and some RVs.

Why the right equipment matters

Weight distribution, vehicle length and access all affect how a medium-duty vehicle should be recovered. Dispatch needs accurate vehicle information so an appropriately rated tow truck and operator can be assigned.

What to tell dispatch

Share the vehicle type, approximate weight if known, whether it rolls or steers, the pickup location, any clearance restrictions and the destination. Those details help prevent delays and equipment mismatches.
